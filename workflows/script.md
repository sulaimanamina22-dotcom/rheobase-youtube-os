# Script: research, write, check every claim

## 1. Research

Go to primary sources: papers, official data, textbooks, and expert explanations from institutions. Save `research.md` in the episode folder with:
- the claims we'll need, each with its source link and the exact place it's supported (page, figure, section or timestamp);
- the strongest counterargument or limitation, plus what's still unknown;
- reference images or diagrams for drawing subjects accurately. These are for our eyes only and never go in the video.

## 2. The scene chain (before writing narration)

Write `chain.md`, the backbone of the episode. For each step:
- the idea and the question it raises next;
- what's on screen and what the subject is doing;
- **how it transforms into the next scene** (zoom through what, morph from what shape).

Check that the chain flows, that every transition is connected, and that the whole thing honestly runs 10+ minutes on substance. If a step can't be drawn without text, redesign it now.

## 3. Write the script

`script.md`, written for Owen's voice:
- Open on the simple question within the first 15 seconds. No channel intro.
- Talk to one curious friend: plain words, unfamiliar terms explained, short sentences that are easy to say aloud.
- Each section ends by opening the next question (the Vsauce chain). Land on how it works and why it could be huge (Cleo), with the limits said honestly.
- Next to each narration paragraph, note the visual and its transition, so the picture and the words line up.
- Write about 145 words per minute of target runtime. Ten minutes is roughly 1,450+ words.
- No em dashes. Nothing that needs on-screen text to make sense.
- An optional closing line asking viewers to subscribe or comment is fine. Keep it short and in Owen's voice.

## 4. Fact-check every claim

The creator's standing rule, from September 13, 2026: fact-check every claim, so that there's no false information, before the script goes to them for approval.

1. List every factual claim in the script, including numbers, dates, names, mechanisms, comparisons, and anything a visual implies. Split sentences that hold several claims.
2. Check each claim against a source you actually opened, and record where it's supported. A citation isn't proof until you've read the supporting passage.
3. Check the math, the units, and whether the source shows a cause, a correlation, an estimate or a possibility.
4. Fix or cut anything unsupported. Adding "might" doesn't rescue an unsupported claim.
5. Reread the final script in full and make sure every claim is in the record.

Save `fact-check.md` as a table: claim, source and location, finding, final wording.

## 5. Creator approval

Give the creator the clean script, the chain and the fact-check summary, including what was corrected and what's uncertain. **Wait for their explicit approval of that exact version.** If the script changes after approval, check the changes and get approval again. Record the approval in `state.md`.
